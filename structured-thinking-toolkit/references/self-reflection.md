# Self-Reflection

Use only when the user explicitly asks for structured reflection about strengths, career direction, or life design. Treat it as hypothesis generation grounded in the user's experiences, not diagnosis, therapy, destiny, or a substitute for qualified support.

Keep a warm but evidence-seeking rhythm: one question, the user's answer, a short reflection, then the next question. Anchor every interpretation to a concrete experience. Mark weakly supported patterns as possibilities and invite correction.

## Strength Discovery

Explore evidence across these branches as relevant:

- activities pursued before external rewards dominated;
- tasks the user finds unusually easy that peers find difficult;
- work that produces energy versus depletion;
- recurring criticism, envy, or overused strengths that may reveal a suppressed capability.

Stop when each proposed strength has a specific evidence chain. Produce strengths with supporting experiences, shadow sides, enabling environments, failure environments, compatible work and collaboration patterns, realistic constraints, and a 30-day low-cost test.

## Life Design

Begin with the current position: health, work, play, and relationships, plus the most urgent concern. Separate gravity problems that cannot currently be changed from designable problems that can be reframed and tested. Explore work values, life values, energizing experiences, draining experiences, and tensions between stated values and actual behavior.

Generate three genuinely different multi-year directions:

1. continuation of the current path;
2. an alternative if the current path disappeared;
3. a path less constrained by status, money, or other people's approval.

Treat each as a prototype, not a prediction. For each, include a short title, indicative timeline, open questions, required resources, and ratings for appeal, confidence, coherence, and feasibility. Finish with a conversation, one-day to one-week prototype, a first step, and a failure-resilient lesson the user can retain.

Reduce scope and encourage qualified human support when the user describes acute distress, danger, abuse, or a mental-health crisis.
