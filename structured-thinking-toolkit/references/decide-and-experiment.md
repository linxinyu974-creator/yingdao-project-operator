# Decide And Experiment

## Bidirectional Steelman

Use when two or more options remain defensible and the decisive variable is unclear.

Restate the user's actual choice, goal, and constraints. Build the strongest case for each option, including fit conditions, upside, downside, and the strongest objection. Identify where the cases genuinely diverge, which assumptions drive that divergence, and the single missing fact most likely to change the result.

Ask one question about that decisive fact when needed. Then provide a conditional judgment, reasons, applicable conditions, and next action. Preserve the user's values; do not manufacture false balance when evidence strongly favors one side.

## Minimum Experiment

Use when discussion has reached diminishing returns and a real-world signal can reduce uncertainty.

List the three assumptions the choice depends on and select the one most likely to change the conclusion. Design the cheapest reversible experiment that provides an observable signal within the user's acceptable time and resource budget.

Specify:

1. the action;
2. time and resources;
3. the observed metric or evidence;
4. the continuation threshold;
5. the stop or reconsideration threshold;
6. the information retained even if the experiment fails;
7. the first action that can begin immediately.

Avoid experiments that create material safety, legal, financial, employment, or relationship risk without explicit user authorization and appropriate safeguards.
